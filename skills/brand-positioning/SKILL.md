# Brand Positioning

## Purpose
Define the strategic posture of the landing page so the interface feels authored, differentiated and coherent with the offer.

## Why This Matters
Many AI-assisted pages collapse into generic tone, vague value statements and interchangeable design language. This skill prevents that by forcing sharper positioning choices.

## Use This Skill When
- The offer is clear but the point of view is weak.
- The page needs a stronger identity than "clean SaaS landing page".
- The copy sounds technically correct but emotionally flat.
- Visual references exist but no clear brand interpretation has been defined.

## Questions To Resolve
- What is the offer really competing against?
- What does the audience already believe?
- What tension should the page amplify?
- What kind of authority should the brand project?
- Which visual and verbal signals would make the page instantly recognizable?

## Positioning Outputs
- Brand posture in one sentence.
- Point of view in one paragraph.
- Three to five thematic words that guide the page.
- What the brand should never sound or look like.
- A contrast statement against category clichés.

## Positioning Lenses
Choose one dominant direction:
- Precise and expert
- Bold and disruptive
- Warm and human
- Premium and controlled
- Experimental and future-facing
- Operational and outcome-driven

## Visual Translation Rules
- Typography should reinforce the posture, not merely remain readable.
- Color should create atmosphere and memory, not just contrast.
- Motion should reveal confidence, not compensate for weak hierarchy.
- Imagery should deepen the concept, not fill empty space.

## Anti-Generic Checks
- Would this still make sense if applied to a random SaaS product?
- Are the claims too broad to be memorable?
- Is the page using category language without interpretation?
- Are visual references being copied instead of translated?
